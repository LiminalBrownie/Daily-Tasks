Why there's no goal line here

Daily weight is mostly water. The bold line is a smoothed trend — that's the real signal, and learning to tell it from the noise is the whole point of weighing often. There's deliberately no target, no streak and no colour-coding: those are the tracker features most associated with disordered eating, and none of them make the trend more accurate.

Creatine and the scale

Creatine pulls water into muscle, so expect 2–4 lb to appear in the first fortnight. It is not fat. If you started creatine and a deficit together, ignore the scale entirely until week three — the trend line will be meaningless before then.

New task

Choosing a kind

Anchor
Small, same trigger every time, and you want it to eventually run itself. Stretching, creatine, lights out. Judge it by: did it happen? These can be daily, and there can be plenty of them — each one is cheap. Retire them once they're automatic.
Practice
A skill you're trying to get better at. Violin, drawing, writing. It will never become automatic, because complex activities need conscious engagement every time — that's normal, not failure. Judge it by: is the difficulty rising? Give it a weekly floor rather than a daily demand, and a current focus rather than just a name.
Project
You return to it in bursts, on no fixed schedule. A campaign, a build, a series. Judge it by: am I still in contact with it? Tracked by rhythm and hours, never by completion rate.

Make it a floor, not a ceiling. "Eat protein" works. "Stay under X" creates pressure to restrict and can only be failed retroactively, after the food is already eaten.

Be specific enough to start. "Write" is a wish; "300 words on the scenario" is a plan. Vagueness is what turns a pursuit into a chore.

Sessions per week you want guaranteed. Anything beyond this counts toward the shared pool. Skill decay is slow — half of gains take months to fade — so the floor is about staying in contact, not preventing loss.

Days. A rhythm, not a deadline.

Progressive difficulty is what drives improvement — practising at a constant level maintains a skill without moving it. The app nudges you if this hasn't changed in three weeks.

Naming the moment raises follow-through (d ≈ 0.59 for health behaviours; smaller when measured objectively, but real). Anchor to a fixed event, not a clock time. Add alternatives if the slot moves — a plan for each realistic context beats one plan you can only follow some days.

A coping plan, distinct from the when/where above. Naming the barrier and your response in advance predicts behaviour at four weeks and six months, but not at two — it's what keeps a habit alive after the novelty wears off, which is precisely when college will test it.
